AI & Beyond

The Algorithmic Eye (AI Perception & Machine Vision)

AI systems now have the ability to interpret and generate images which may consist of humans, identifying patterns or objects, and even background/scenes. These models have altered how information is categorised, recognised, and understood across varying digital platforms. As a machine/generative model's vision increasingly learns and expands with its knowledge, it can begin to influence and restructure what it deems as being significant in everyday life.

Synthetic Images & Manipulation (Deepfakes, Generative AI)

Advanced image-generation tools now have the ability to create realistic visuals without a camera, challenging what we see as being authentic. The rise and improvement of deepfakes and AI-manipulated imagery has been so effective, it now blurs the boundary between what we classify as being real or fabricated. This dramatically alters how people trust and interpret visual imagery/evidence, while redefining what it means for an image to “prove” or represent something.

  • AI can create images that are seperate from our physical reality
  • The rise in deepfakes complicates trust in all visual forms of media

Futures of Photography (Ethics, Sustainability & New Possibilities)

As imaging technology evolves, photography’s future involves balancing innovation with creative responsibility. With lingering questions and concerns around data ethics and environmental impact, these factors will determine and shape how new tools are designed and used. A safe assumption we can make about photography is that it will continue to move beyond capturing moments, and will continue to influence our socio-economic systems of identity, culture, digital experience, and perception.

  • Ethical concerns around the use of AI alter it’s imaging systems
  • Photography's breadth widens with its integration with virtual, augmented, and AI-enhanced realities
